solder is very hot, melting tem pis twice that of water. Looks the same as cold metal.

Step 1: heat up your iron
315-370 degrees C, or 600-700 degrees F

Step 2:
make connections mechanically stable, slightly bend leads of through-hole components, or use sticky tape.

Step 3: clean your iron
remove oxidation or excess solder, can result in poorly soldered joints. Rosin core solder has a small amount of rosin in its centre, which acts as a flux and eats away the oxides

Step 4: apply heat and solder
Heat the joint evenly and let the solder flow onto it, don't put solder directly onto the iron. ensure good contact between iron and joint. joint needs time.

Step 5: inspect the joint
did the solder wet to both surfaces? is the joint smooth and shiny? is there too much/too little solder?
on a printed circuit board, the connection should make a volcano shape, adhering smoothly both the pad and component lead. Cut component leads if needed.
