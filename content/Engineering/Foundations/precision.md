A metric used in reporting [[uncertainty]], alongside [[accuracy]].

> [!NOTE]
> A measurement of the spread between different datapoints or the level of [[uncertainty]] in a final reported value.

Whether a dataset is "precise" is context dependent.

Typically, results quoted to 1-2 [[Significant figures]] (down to $\pm$ 5% of the result) would be considered "low precision", while more than 4 [[Significant figures]]\(less than 0.01% [[uncertainty]]) would be considered "high precision".

Precision can be implied when not explicitly stated:

- A reported distance of d = 2.374 metres implies that the true distance should be within the range 2.3735 < d < 2.3745.
- If the true distance could be anywhere within a wider range such as 2.23 < d < 2.45, then the result would be better reported as d = 2.
- For example, how far is the Clayton Campus Centre from the bus loop? “About 350m” is a lot more sensible than “345.678982912m”
