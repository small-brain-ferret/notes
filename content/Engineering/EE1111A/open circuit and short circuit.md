| Condition     |        Voltage |        Current |
| ------------- | -------------: | -------------: |
| Open circuit  | can be nonzero |          $I=0$ |
| Short circuit |          $V=0$ | can be nonzero |
An **open circuit** is a broken path.

$$
\boxed{I = 0}
$$

Voltage across the open circuit can still be non-zero.

A **short circuit** is an ideal wire with zero resistance.

$$
\boxed{V = 0}
$$

Current can still flow through the short circuit.

## Killing Independent Sources

When using [[the principle of superposition]] or finding $R_{Th}$, independent sources may be set to zero.

### Voltage Source

Setting a voltage source to zero means:

$$
V = 0
$$

A wire also has zero voltage across it.
Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Voltage source} \rightarrow \text{short circuit}}
$$

### Current Source

Setting a current source to zero means:

$$
I = 0
$$

An open circuit has zero current.
Therefore:

$$
\boxed{\text{Current source} \rightarrow \text{open circuit}}
$$
