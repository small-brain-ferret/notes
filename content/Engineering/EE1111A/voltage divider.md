> [!summary]
> **Voltage divider = series circuit**
>
> In a series circuit, the **same current** flows through every resistor, but the **voltage is divided** between the resistors.

## Main idea

The total source voltage is shared between the resistors.

$$
V_s = V_1 + V_2 + \cdots
$$

A bigger resistor gets a bigger share of the voltage.

## Formula

$$
V_x = V_s \frac{R_x}{R_1 + R_2 + \cdots}
$$

where:

- $V_x$ = voltage across the resistor you care about
- $V_s$ = source / total voltage
- $R_x$ = resistor you care about
- $R_1 + R_2 + \cdots$ = total resistance of all resistors in series

> [!tip]
> Think of it as:
>
> $$
> \boxed{
> V_{\text{wanted}}
> =
> V_{\text{total}}
> \times
> \frac{R_{\text{wanted}}}{R_{\text{total}}}
> }
> $$

> [!important]
> Voltage divider works when the resistors are in **series** and therefore carry the same current.

## Quick memory

$$
\boxed{\text{Series} \rightarrow \text{Voltage Divider}}
$$

Bigger resistance $\rightarrow$ bigger voltage drop.
