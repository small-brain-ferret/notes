> [!summary]
> **Current divider = parallel circuit**
>
> In a parallel circuit, the **same voltage** is across each branch, but the **current is divided** between the branches.

## Main idea

The total current entering a node splits into the parallel branches.

$$
I_T = I_1 + I_2 + \cdots
$$

A smaller resistance gets more current.

## Formula

For two resistors in parallel:

$$
I_1
=
I_T
\frac{R_2}{R_1 + R_2}
$$

$$
I_2
=
I_T
\frac{R_1}{R_1 + R_2}
$$

where:

- $I_T$ = total current entering the parallel branches
- $I_1$ = current through $R_1$
- $I_2$ = current through $R_2$
- $R_1$, $R_2$ = resistance of each branch

> [!important]
> The resistor in the numerator is the **other resistor**.
>
> To find $I_1$, use $R_2$ on top.
>
> To find $I_2$, use $R_1$ on top.

> [!important]
> Current divider works when the resistors are in **parallel**.

## Why does the smaller resistor get more current?

Using Ohm's law:

$$
I = \frac{V}{R}
$$

In parallel, each branch has the same voltage.
Therefore:

$$
R \downarrow
\Rightarrow
I \uparrow
$$

So:

$$
\boxed{\text{smaller resistance} \rightarrow \text{more current}}
$$

## Quick memory

$$
\boxed{\text{Parallel} \rightarrow \text{Current Divider}}
$$

Smaller resistance $\rightarrow$ larger current.
