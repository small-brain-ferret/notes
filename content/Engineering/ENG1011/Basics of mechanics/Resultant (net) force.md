A body may be subjected to multiple [[force]] ($F_{1}$, $F_2$, etc).

The sum of these forces is the resultant force, $F_R$, or net force, described as a [[Vector(s)]] with a magnitude and direction.

For [[equilibrium]], $F_R$ = 0.

![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-11 at 12.07.32.png]]

A resultant force can be determined via [[Graphical vector addition]].

The orthogonal components of a resultant force can be determined using [[vector decomposition]].

These components of the [[Resultant (net) force]] can be determined via the additions for the respective components.

> [!NOTE] Components of the resultant force
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F_{R,\ x}=\sum F_{x} \\
> F_{R,\ y}=\sum F_{y}
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> eg:
> ![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-11 at 13.23.19.png]]
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> F_{R,\ x}= F_{1,\ x}+F_{2,\ x}+F_{3,\ x} \\
> F_{R,\ y}= F_{1, \ y} + F_{2, \ y} + F_{3, \ y}
> \end{align}
> $$

> [!NOTE] Angle to the horizontal of the resultant force
>
> $$
> \tan(\theta)=\lvert \frac{F_{R,\ y}}{F_{R,\ x}}\rvert
> $$
>
> ![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-11 at 13.25.27.png]]

# Example: determining magnitude and angle of $F_{R}$

Determine the resultant vector and the angle $\alpha$ it makes with the horizontal.

![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-11 at 13.31.39.png]]

#### Step 1: Use vector decomposition to determine the orthogonal components of $F_R$

$$
\begin{align}
F_{R,\ x} & =\sum F_{x} \\ 
 & = 2\cos(45^\circ)-6\cos(60^\circ) \\
  & =-1.5858\text{ kN} \\
   \\
  
F_{R,\ y} & =\sum F_{y} \\
 & =-2\sin(45^\circ)-6\sin(60^\circ) \\
  & =-6.6104\text{ kN}
\end{align}
$$

#### Step 2: Use the components to determine the magnitude for $F_R$

$$
\begin{align}
\lvert  F_{R}  \rvert & =\sqrt{ (F_{R, \ x})^2 + (F_{R, \ y})^2 } \\
 & =6.7979 \text{ kN}
\end{align}
$$

#### Step 3: Using $\tan(\theta)=\lvert \frac{F_{R,\ y}}{F_{R,\ x}}\rvert$, determine the angle

$$
\begin{align} \\
\tan(\theta) & =\left\lvert  \frac{F_{R,\ y}}{F_{R,\ x}} \right\rvert \\
\alpha & =\frac{-6.6104}{-1.5858} \\
 & =76.51^\circ
\end{align}
$$
