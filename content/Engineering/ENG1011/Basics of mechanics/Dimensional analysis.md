# Example - determining [[Dimensional homogeneity]]

Perform dimensional analysis on the equation $s=ut+\frac{1}{2} a t^2$ and justify its [[Dimensional homogeneity]].

#### Step 1: List all physical quantities and their corresponding [[Unit dimension(s)]].

$t$: time (s) $T$
$u$: initial velocity (m/s) $\frac{[L]}{[T]}$
$a$: acceleration (m/s$^2$) $\frac{[L]}{[T]^2}$

#### Step 2: substitute the unit dimensions of each variable into the given equation.

Note: constants are non-dimensional.

$$
\begin{align}
s & =ut+\frac{1}{2} a t^2 \\
[L] & =\frac{[L]}{[T]}[T]+[1] \frac{[L]}{[T]^2}[T]^2
\end{align}
$$

#### Step 3: Simplify each side

$$
\begin{align}
 LHS & =[L] \\
 RHS & =[L]+[1][L] \\
  & =[L]
\end{align}
$$

# Example: determine dimensions and units of a variable

Determine the unit dimension and unit of Reynolds number in $Re=\frac{{\rho \mu L}}{\mu}$.

#### Step 1: List all physical quantities and their corresponding [[Unit dimension(s)]].

$Re$: Reynolds number (?)
$\rho$: density (kg/m$^3$)  $\frac{[M]}{[L]^3}$
$u$: velocity (m/s) $\frac{[L]}{[T]}$
$y$: characteristic length (m) $[L]$
$mu$: viscosity (kg/m$\cdot$s) $\frac{[M]}{[L][T]}$

#### Step 2: substitute the unit dimensions of each variable into the given equation.

Note: constants are non-dimensional.

$$
\begin{align}
Re & =\frac{{\rho \mu L}}{\mu} \\
[Re] & =\frac{[M]}{[L]^3} \frac{[L]}{[T]}[L] \frac{[L][T]}{[M]}
\end{align}
$$

#### Step 3: Simplify the required side

$$
\begin{align}
 [Re]=[1]
\end{align}
$$

$\therefore$ the Reynolds number is dimensionless.
