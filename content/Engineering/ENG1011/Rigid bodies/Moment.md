> [!NOTE] Definition
> When a [[force]] is applied to a body, it will produce a tendency for the body to rotate about a point that is not on the line of action of the force.
>
> This tendency to rotate is often referred to as a moment (or torque).

e.g.
Applying a force $F$ on the wrench handle to rotate the wrench about the $z$-axis to unscrew the bolt at point $O$. That is, we're causing a moment about point $O$.

![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-16 at 18.26.17.png]]

> [!NOTE] Magnitude of the moment
> The magnitude of the moment (M$_O$) is directly proportional to the magnitude of the applied force ($F$) and the perpendicular distance ($d$) from the axis of rotation to the line of action of the force.
>
> The perpendicular distance is also known as the [[moment arm]].
>
> $$
> M_{O}=Fd \sin(\theta)
> $$

![[Attachments/Screenshot 2026-06-16 at 18.32.20.png]]

> [!NOTE] Direction of the moment
> Defined by its [[moment axis]], which is perpendicular to the plane containing $F$ and $d$.
>
> In 2D, the moment direction is represented by a curl notation (blue arrow).
>
> ![[Attachments/Pasted image 20260616203243.png]]
>
> We will generally consider positive moments as counter-clockwise since they are directed along the positive $z$-axis (out of the page). Clockwise moments are negative.
