Recognising two and [[three-force members]] is critical in [[equilibrium of a rigid body]] because they allow us to simplify the analysis of a system.

> [!NOTE]
> Member that experiences forces only at their two frictionless ends and carry no [[load]] in between.
>
> Don't have to be straight.
>
> If straight, the members will only experience [[axial tension]] or [[axial compression]] forces.
>
> If curved or kinked, they will experience a combination of axial, [[shear]], and [[bending]] forces.
>
> A two force member only has 2 forces applied.
>
> Thus, [[equilibrium of a rigid body]] is achieved if the 2 forces:
>
> - are equal and opposite (force equilibrium)
> - share the same line of action, which only occurs along the line joining the points of applied forces (moment equilibrium)
>
> This means that two-force members in equilibrium can only be in tension or compression.

a [[two-force members|two-force member]] in [[equilibrium of a rigid body|equilibrium]] is always an [[axially loaded member]].
