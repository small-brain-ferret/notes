> [!NOTE]
> Also known as Euler-Bernoulli Beam theory.
>
> Assumptions:
>
> 1. A [[beam]] is long relative to its depth and width. Thus, [[stress]] developed perpendicular to the beam length are much smaller than those parallel, and can be ignored
> 2. The beam cross-section is constant along its length
> 3. The beam is symmetrical about the yy axis, thus no twisting or torsion
> 4. Deflections/deformations are small
> 5. The material is [[isotropic]] and obeys [[Hooke's law]]
> 6. Plane sections remain plane. This is true when a beam is subject to pure [[bending]], and experiences zero [[shear]] deformation
>
> Real-life structures never meet these assumptions exactly, but usually approximate them well enough for the theory to be fairly accurate.
>
> It considers:
>
> - the type of material (eg. [[Young's Modulus E]])
> - the way the beam deforms (curves)
> - the geometry
> - the interal equilibrium
