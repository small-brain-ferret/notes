Atomic bonding describes the attractive forces that hold atoms together within a material.

The type and strength of bonding affect many [[material property|material properties]].

Different classes of materials have very different [[Young's Modulus E]] because their atoms are held together by different bonds.

|Material class|Typical stiffness|
|---|--:|
|Ceramics|Very high|
|Metals|High|
|Polymers|Low|
|Elastomers|Very low|

> [!NOTE]
> Differences in [[Young's Modulus E]] between material classes are largely caused by differences in the **strength of their atomic bonding**.

The four main types of bonding are:

1. [[ionic bonding]]
2. [[covalent bonding]]
3. [[metallic bonding]]
4. [[secondary bonding]]

| Bond type | Relative strength/Energy | Directionality       | Commonly found in                                  |
| --------- | ------------------------ | -------------------- | -------------------------------------------------- |
| Ionic     | High                     | Non-directional      | Ceramics                                           |
| Covalent  | High                     | Directional          | Ceramics, semiconductors and within polymer chains |
| Metallic  | Variable                 | Non-directional      | Metals                                             |
| Secondary | Low                      | Depends on bond type | Between [[polymer chains]]                         |
