> [!NOTE]
> Consists of positively charged metal-ion cores surrounded by mobile, delocalised $e^-$.
>
> This is often described as:
> a lattice of positive ions embedded in a sea of electrons.
>
> The electrons are not restricted to one particular pair of atoms and can move throughout the metallic structure.

> [!NOTE] Strength of metals
> The strength of metallic bonding varies considerably between metals.

| Metal     | Relative metallic bond strength |
| --------- | ------------------------------- |
| Tungsten  | Very strong                     |
| Steel     | Strong                          |
| Aluminium | Moderate                        |
| Mercury   | Weak                            |

> Explaining why metals can have very different stiffness ([[Young's Modulus E]]) and [[melting point|melting points]].

> [!NOTE] electrical conductivity of metals
> Metals have high [[electrical conductivity]] because their delocalised electrons can move through the material.
>
> When an electrical potential is applied, the mobile electrons transport electrical charge.
>
> The high mobility of electrons in metallic bonds explains why metals are generally good electrical conductors.

> [!NOTE] thermal conductivity of metals
> Mobile electrons can also transport thermal energy through a metal.
>
> This contributes to the high [[thermal conductivity]] of many metals.
