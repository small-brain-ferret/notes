> [!NOTE]
> During [[plastic deformation at an atomic level|plastic deformation]], or as we enter the [[plastic region]], the number of [[dislocations]] dramatically increases. This process is called work hardening.
>
> The distance between dislocations decreases. Dislocation density increases.

> [!NOTE] Mechanism for how a sample is strengthened with work hardening
> As we plastically deform a sample, then remove the load, the dimensions will change.
>
> If the sample is reloaded, the stress-strain relationship is linear.
> It would not yield at the original [[yield stress|yield strength]], but would go back up to where [[stress]] was originally released.
>
> Thus, the [[yield stress]] has been increased from $\sigma_{YS}$ to $\sigma_{A}$.
>
> We have strengthened the material through work hardening.
>
> - [[yield stress|yield strength]] ($\sigma$) increases
> - [[Ultimate Tensile Strength]] (TS) increases
> - [[ductility]] (%EL or %AR) decreases
