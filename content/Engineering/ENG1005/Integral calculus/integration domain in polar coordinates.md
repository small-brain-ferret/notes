> [!NOTE]
> when an integration domain $D$ is defined in [[polar coordinates]] by inequalities of the form $g(\theta)\leq r\leq h(\theta)$ and $\alpha\leq \theta\leq \beta$ ($0\leq \beta-\alpha\leq{2}\pi$).
>
> $$
> \int _{D}\int f(x,y) \, dA = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \int_{g(\theta)}^{h(\theta)} f(r\cos \theta,r\sin \theta)r \, dr  \, d\theta 
> $$
>
> ![[Attachments/8f7fb2dbdd906e85601a79d183142f4e 1.jpg]]

> [!NOTE] Special case - circle or any sector of a circle
> When $g(\theta)=0$.
>
> $$
> \int _{D}\int f(x,y) \, dA = \int_{\alpha}^{\beta} \int_{0}^{h(\theta)} f(r\cos \theta,r\sin \theta)r \, dr  \, d\theta 
> $$
>
> ![[Attachments/b6b3a537b4ec81f56191874038c28930.jpg]]

Useful for [[iterated integrals]].
