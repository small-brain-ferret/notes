> [!NOTE] polar coordinates
> ![[Attachments/367e6cccfc06ae62ba33efb6a32504a2.jpg]]
>
> $P(x,y)$ can be represented by the ordered pair $(r,\theta)$, where
>
> $$
> \theta=\begin{cases}
> \alpha & \text{if }y\geq{0} \\
> -\alpha & \text{if }y<{0}
> \end{cases}
> $$
>
> The origin $O$ is called the **pole**
> The positive $x$-axis is called the **polar axis**
> $(r,\theta)$ is the **polar coordinates** of the point **P**

> [!NOTE]
> For any integer $n$,
> $(r,\theta)$ and $(r, \theta+2n\pi)$ (rotate OP through $n$ complete revolutions - back to the same point) represent the same point. For uniqueness, we shall restrict the domain for $\theta$ to $-\pi<\theta<\pi$.

> [!NOTE] polar -> cartesian
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> x & =r\cos(\theta) \\
> y & =r\sin(\theta ) \\
> r^2 & =x^2+y^2
> \end{align}
> $$
>
> $$
> \begin{align}
> r=\sqrt{ x^2+y^2 } \\
> \tan(\theta)=\frac{y}{x}
> \end{align}
> $$
