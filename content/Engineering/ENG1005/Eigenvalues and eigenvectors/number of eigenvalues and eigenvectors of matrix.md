> [!NOTE] $2$x$2$ matrices
> For $2$x$2$ matrices with real entries, we can have:
>
> - 2 real, distinct [[Eigenvalue(s)]], each with a single [[Eigenvector(s)]] direction
> - 1 repeated eigenvalue with a single eigenvector direction
> - a pair of complex conjugates $\lambda_{r}\pm i \lambda_{i}$ with complex conjugate vectors.
> - one (repeated) eigenvalue where every vector is an eigenvector.

> [!NOTE] $n$x$n$ matrices
> For an $n$x$n$ matrix, the [[characteristic polynomial]] wil will have degree of $n$.
>
> It can be shown that there are always $n$ roots of the characteristic equation and so $n$ eigenvalues of the matrix, but some may be repeated.
>
> The number of times an eigenvalue is repeated is known as its [[algebraic multiplicity]].

> [!NOTE] Eigenvalues, Eigenvectors & Multiplicity
> Non-repeated eigenvalues have a single eigenvector direction.
> Repeated eigenvalues can have either:
>
> - a single eigenvector direction, or
> - multiple (infinitely many) directions.
>
> Instead of counting directions, we count the number of **linearly independent eigenvectors** (see [[linear dependency]]), called the [[geometric multiplicity]].
>
> $$
> 1 \le \text{geometric multiplicity} \le \text{algebraic multiplicity}
> $$
>
> If $A$ is a real matrix, then eigenvalues are either:
>
> - real, or
> - complex conjugate pairs $\lambda_r \pm i\lambda_i$, with corresponding eigenvectors also forming conjugate pairs.
